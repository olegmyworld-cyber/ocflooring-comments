Floors here move with the seasons

A long wet season and a genuinely dry summer swing indoor humidity enough that solid wood expands and contracts on a cycle you can measure. A gap that looks alarming in late August can be half its width by December — which is why we won't pack a gap at its widest. Filler pushed in during the open season gets crushed and spat back out when the boards swell.

The material decides the fix

Older housing in both counties is solid hardwood — vertical-grain Douglas fir in pre-war houses, strip oak in post-war ones — and that sands and blends. The newer Eastside build-out and the subdivisions spreading north are engineered hardwood, laminate and LVP with wear layers thinner than a credit card. Those don't sand: the fix is plank replacement, and the hard part is sourcing a product that may be discontinued.

Every city we serve has its own floor repair page covering the housing there and the failures that keep coming up. If yours is on the list, start there — and if it isn't, the same crew and warranty apply anywhere in King or Snohomish County.

Why Homeowners Call Us Before They Replace a Floor

Most flooring companies treat repair as the small job they squeeze in between installs. We treat it as the work — and that changes what you get.

It's a matching problem first

Pulling a ruined board and fastening a new one down is the easy half. Getting the species, board width, grain, stain and sheen to agree with a floor that has been quietly ageing for thirty years is the half that decides whether you notice the patch every day — or forget within a week where it used to be.

Repair and refinish, one crew

We repair hardwood, laminate and vinyl plank — boards and planks one at a time, gaps that stay open, pet and water damage, lifted thresholds. And because we also refinish floors, we blend the repaired area into the surrounding finish instead of leaving a bright new rectangle in your living room.

Straight answers before work starts

Because we refinish too, we'll tell you when a repair is the wrong answer. Sometimes that's the cheaper call, sometimes the more expensive one — either way you get it in writing before anyone starts work.
