    Recoat or Full Refinish? The 60-Second Everett Floor Check

    This is the single most expensive question on this page, because the answer is either $1.99 or $3.99+ per square foot. Grab a glass of water, walk to the most worn spot on your floor (usually the kitchen sink, the back door, or the hallway to the bedrooms), and answer five questions.

    Recoat or Refinish?

    Answer honestly. The verdict updates as you go.

    1. Put a few drops of water on the worn spot. Does it bead up and sit there for a minute?
    2. Are there scratches that show bare, lighter wood, or boards that have turned gray?
    3. Any dark black or brown spots from pets, plants or water?
    4. Do you want to change the color of the floor?
    5. Has anyone used wax, Murphy Oil Soap, Mop & Glo, Rejuvenate or a "floor polish" on it?

    "There's Hardwood Under My Carpet." Can It Be Saved?

    This is the most common Everett call we get, and the answer is usually yes. Carpet protects a hardwood floor from decades of sun and traffic, so what comes out from under 1980s carpet in a Delta or Riverside house is often in better shape than a floor that was exposed the whole time. Here's how to check before you call anyone:

    • Pull a floor vent register. Look at the edge of the cut-out. Hardwood shows layers of tongue-and-groove boards ¾" thick. Plywood subfloor shows thin laminated layers.
    • Lift a corner in a closet. Carpet is usually only tacked at the edges. Peel back a corner and the pad; you'll see the boards.
    • Look for the border. Pre-1940 Everett homes often have a stained or painted border about 12 to 18 inches in from the walls, with a different finish in the middle where the rug sat. That all sands out to one uniform floor.

    What sands out: staple holes, tack-strip nail holes, carpet-pad residue, paint spatter, old shellac and varnish, gray oxidation. What doesn't: black pet stains that reached the wood, boards cut for old floor furnaces or heat registers, and water damage that lifted or cupped the boards. Those are board-replacement repairs, and we weave in matching oak or fir before finishing so the repair disappears. If the floor is more than half damaged, we'll tell you honestly that new hardwood installation is the better use of money.

    Pet Stains, Winter Gaps and Other Everett Repairs

    • Pet urine stains. Gray or light brown usually sands out. Black means the urine reached the wood fibers; sanding won't remove it and neither will bleach. We replace the boards.
    • Gaps between boards every winter. Normal in Everett. Furnace heat drops indoor humidity, boards shrink, gaps open, then close in June. We fill during refinishing, but we won't fill wide seasonal gaps in a fir floor with hard filler, because it pops out by spring.
    • Cupped boards near a door or dishwasher. Find the moisture source first, let the floor dry to match the rest of the house, then sand flat. Sanding a wet, cupped floor flat gives you a crowned floor when it dries.
    • Sun-faded floors by the sliders. Common in west-facing Bayside and Rucker Hill homes. A full sand resets the color; a recoat won't.
    • Old floor-furnace cutouts. We patch with matching species and, on fir, hunt down reclaimed old-growth so the grain matches.

    How to Compare Refinishing Quotes in Everett

    Three quotes for the same floor can come back $2,000 apart. Most of that gap is in what's included, not the sanding. Ask every contractor these questions and you'll be comparing the same job:

    • Price per square foot or per room? Per-room pricing hides the math. Ask for the rate, the minimum, and the measured area.
    • How many coats, and which finish, by brand name? "Three coats of Bona Mega One" is a spec. "Polyurethane" is not.
    • Is dust containment included, or an add-on? Modern sanders capture most dust already; sealed barriers and negative air are the upgrade.
    • What do repairs cost, and are they in the quote? Board replacement should be a line item, not a surprise on Day 1.
    • Are you a registered Washington contractor, bonded and insured? Look up any contractor by name at the WA Department of Labor & Industries. Everett doesn't require a permit to refinish floors, but the contractor still has to be registered with the state.
    • Written warranty? Ours is one year on workmanship. Finish peeling or dust nibs inside that window are on us.
    • Is the crew certified on the finish they're selling? Bona Certified Craftsman status means the crew was trained on the sanding sequence and the finish system, not just the product name. Ask for it by name.

    Can we stay in the house during refinishing?
    Yes, most Everett families do. We use low-odor, low-VOC Bona waterborne finishes and work room by room. You'll stay off freshly coated rooms and plan sleeping arrangements if bedrooms are included.
    There's hardwood under the carpet in my Everett house. Can it be refinished?
    Almost always. Carpet protects hardwood, so floors under 1980s carpet in Riverside, Delta or Lowell homes are often in excellent shape. Staple holes, tack strip holes and pad residue sand out. Black pet stains and cut-out boards are repaired with matching wood.
    My North Everett house has fir floors. Can fir be refinished like oak?
    Yes, and old-growth fir is worth it. It's softer than oak, so it needs a gentler grit sequence and lighter machines, and it looks best with a clear natural finish. Stain on fir blotches unless the wood is conditioned first. The price per square foot is the same as oak.
    Do I need a full refinish or just a recoat?
    If water beads on the floor, there's no bare wood or gray boards, no wax or oil soap has been used, and you like the color, a $1.99/sq ft screen & recoat is enough. Scratches to raw wood, dark stains, wax, or a color change need the full $3.99+ refinish.
    How often should hardwood floors be refinished?
    A full sand every 15–25 years, and a recoat every 4–7 years in between. Solid ¾" hardwood has 4–6 full sandings in it over its life, so recoating on schedule is how you make an Everett floor last a century.
    Does Everett's rainy climate affect refinishing?
    Not the schedule; we work indoors with waterborne finishes that dry in hours. What matters is that the house sits at normal living humidity, about 35–55%, for a couple of weeks before sanding so boards are at their normal width. Run your heat normally in winter.
    Water-based or oil-based finish for a Puget Sound home?
    Water-based (Bona) for nearly every home: fast drying, low odor, stays clear, and it's the only way to get the light natural look. Oil-based ambers over time and keeps you out of the rooms for days. We'll use it on request for vacant properties.
    Can engineered hardwood be refinished?
    Once or twice if the wear layer is 2 mm or more. Thin-veneer engineered floors, common in some 2005+ Everett townhomes, can't take a full sand. We check the wear layer at a floor vent during the free estimate.
    Do I need a permit to refinish hardwood floors in Everett?
    No. Refinishing is cosmetic work and the City of Everett doesn't require a permit for it. Your contractor does need to be registered, bonded and insured with Washington State, which you can verify on the L&I website.
    Is refinishing worth it before selling an Everett home?
    Usually yes. Refinishing costs a fraction of replacement, and freshly finished hardwood photographs better than anything else you can do for the money. A recoat can make a 10-year-old floor listing-ready for under $2/sq ft.
