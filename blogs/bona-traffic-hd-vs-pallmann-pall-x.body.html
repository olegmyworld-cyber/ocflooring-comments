Same Chemistry Family, Different Accents

Strip the branding and you’re looking at close cousins. Both flagship products — Bona Traffic HD and Pallmann’s two-component Pall-X — are waterborne polyurethanes that get mixed with a hardener on site. That crosslinking step is what separates commercial-grade finishes from the single-component products sold at hardware stores: the film that forms is dramatically tougher, chemical-resistant, and stable enough to be specified for restaurants and retail floors. Both dry clear and stay clear — no ambering, ever — which is why either one protects a gray or natural stain the way you chose it. Both come in the usual sheens, and satin remains the crowd favorite on the floors we do.

Pallmann’s catalog runs deeper than one product, which confuses homeowners comparing quotes: Pall-X 96 is the one-component workhorse we might use in a low-traffic bedroom-only job, while the two-component versions are what we put head-to-head against Traffic HD. When a competitor’s quote just says “Pallmann,” it’s fair — and smart — to ask which one.

The Case for Bona Traffic HD

Traffic HD is the benchmark. When architects spec a waterborne finish for a commercial floor, this is usually the name on the drawing, and its reputation is earned: in abrasion testing it leads the category, which is why we called it out in our Traffic HD deep-dive as the finish we default to on the hardest-working floors — kitchens, entries, homes with big dogs. It also carries the GREENGUARD Gold indoor-air certification at roughly 65 g/L VOC, which matters to families who want the paperwork and not just our word that the house is livable during the job.

The honest downsides: it’s one of the most expensive waterborne finishes you can buy, and it is — we say this with affection — boring. It doesn’t level with quite the silky, self-forgiving flow of some competitors; it just goes down, cures hard, and never calls you back. There are worse traits in a floor finish. One more soft factor: name recognition. When you sell the house and the listing says “floors refinished in Bona Traffic HD,” Eastside buyers’ agents know what that means. Pallmann deserves the same nod and doesn’t always get it.

The Case for Pallmann Pall-X

Pallmann is the German engineering answer to the same problem, and finishers who use it tend to love it for how it behaves during application: it flows out and levels beautifully, which shows up in the final floor as fewer visible lap marks and a very even sheen — something you notice most on big open Kirkland great rooms with low afternoon light coming off the lake. Durability on the two-component Pall-X products is genuinely comparable to Traffic HD; we have both out in the field on busy family floors and we are not seeing a difference in how they’re wearing. Our Pallmann overview covers the system in more detail.

Material cost typically runs a bit below Bona’s flagship, which matters to us more than to you — our refinishing price doesn’t change with the brand. The honest downsides: less name recognition with homeowners and agents, and a product line deep enough that “we use Pallmann” can mean the premium two-component system or the budget one-component, depending on who’s quoting. Ask. A contractor who answers precisely is telling you something about the rest of their work too.

What We See on Kirkland Floors

Kirkland is one of our busiest cities, and its housing stock splits neatly into two refinishing personalities. The 1960s–80s ramblers up through Juanita and North Rose Hill usually hide red oak under decades of carpet — solid, forgiving wood that either finish system flatters. The older lake cottages closer to the water are a different story: softer fir, more sun exposure off the lake, and entryways that eat finish because half of summer walks in with sand on its feet. On those floors the finish grade matters more than the finish brand — a two-component system from either maker will outlast a builder-grade product several times over, which is most of what you’re paying for in a professional refinish. Both systems get the same substrate treatment from us: a full sand finished with the Lägler TRIO for flatness, then 2–3 coats with abrasion between. If you’re weighing a project, our Kirkland refinishing page has the local specifics, and the full numbers live in our refinishing cost guide — natural finish runs $3.99/sq ft with either brand on the truck.

Bona vs Pallmann: What Kirkland Homeowners Ask Us

Is Bona Traffic HD more durable than Pallmann?
In laboratory abrasion testing Traffic HD leads the waterborne category. In real houses, on the two-component Pallmann products, we cannot see a wear difference between them after years in the field. Both dramatically outlast one-component hardware-store finishes, which is the gap that actually matters.
Do you charge more for one brand than the other?
No. A natural refinish is $3.99 per square foot with either system, and a stain refinish is $6.50. The brands price differently to us, but we quote the job, not the bucket.
Which finish dries faster?
They're effectively identical: recoat in hours, socks the same evening as the final coat, furniture back at 48 to 72 hours, rugs after about a week, full cure in roughly 7 days. Neither brand buys you a faster week.
Can you put Bona finish over a Pallmann sealer, or mix the systems?
We don't. Each manufacturer tests and warranties its sealers and finishes as a system, and adhesion between coats is the single most common failure point in floor finishing. Whichever brand starts your floor finishes it.
Which is safer for kids and pets in the house?
Both are low-VOC waterborne finishes and most of our clients stay home during the work. Traffic HD carries GREENGUARD Gold certification at roughly 65 g/L, which is the strongest paperwork in the category if indoor air quality is your deciding factor.
Does either brand yellow over time?
No. Both are non-yellowing waterborne finishes — the color you see when we finish is the color you keep. If your current floor has gone orange, that's an old oil-modified polyurethane, not either of these.
How many coats do you apply?
Two to three depending on traffic level and the product, with abrasion between coats for adhesion. That's the same system architecture for both brands, applied over a full sand with a Lägler TRIO final pass for flatness.
Which one would you put in your own house?
Traffic HD in the kitchen and entry, honestly — the wear-test record buys peace of mind where floors suffer most. In bedrooms and offices we'd flip a coin, and the coin would land on whichever crew's favorite was on the truck. That's how close this is.
