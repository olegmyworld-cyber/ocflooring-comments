The Half of the Floor You Never See

Every quote we write has a line for pad, and it is the line people skim. Understandable — you will never see it again after install morning — but the pad is doing a job every minute the carpet is on the floor. It absorbs the impact of every footstep so the carpet backing does not have to. It insulates the room and quiets it. It is the reason carpet feels like carpet instead of fabric glued to plywood. And it is the difference between a carpet that looks tired at year four and the same carpet looking fine at year ten: without enough support underneath, the backing flexes on every step, the tufts crush in the lanes, and the floor ages at double speed. That failure mode — a good carpet murdered by a cheap pad — is one of the most common things we tear out, and how long carpet lasts is largely a story about what was under it.

Thickness vs Density: Buy the Number You Cannot Feel in the Store

Pad has two specs. Thickness is the one everyone asks about, because it is the one hands can measure. Density — how much material is packed into each cubic foot, quoted in pounds — is the one that matters, because it decides whether the pad still pushes back after a hundred thousand footsteps. A thick, low-density pad feels wonderful in a showroom squeeze and collapses in the hallway within a couple of years, taking the carpet's support with it. A slightly thinner, denser pad feels firmer on day one and feels exactly the same on day three thousand. When only one spec fits in your head, keep density.

And resist the instinct that more inches equal more luxury. Past about half an inch of cushion, the carpet above starts to flex like a trampoline skin: seams work themselves visible, the power-stretch loosens sooner (ripples are a stretching problem, as our restretching guide explains, but over-thick pad helps cause them), and stair installations get outright spongy. Several carpet manufacturers cap the pad thickness their warranties accept — usually right around that half-inch line, thinner still for berber and other looped styles, which want a firm, dense, low-profile pad so the loops stay supported. The right answer is almost never the thickest pad on the rack; it is the densest pad at the correct thickness for your carpet.

Moisture-Barrier Pads, Explained Without the Marketing

An ordinary pad is an open sponge. A spilled glass, a puppy's mistake, a toddler's science experiment — liquid is through the carpet in seconds and into that sponge, which holds it against the subfloor where no towel, rented machine or professional wand can reach. That is why a cleaned carpet can smell fine for a week and then bloom again on a humid day: the smell was never in the carpet. A moisture-barrier pad adds a sealed top layer, so the accident pools on top of the pad, inside the carpet, where cleaning actually works.

We treat barrier pad as non-negotiable under pet-household carpet, and strongly recommend it for young families, dining rooms and finished basements — anywhere the first bad spill is a matter of when. Two honest notes: it protects the floor system, not the carpet's surface, so pair it with a sensible fiber (our stain-resistance guide sorts that aisle), and it is not a waterproofing system — a basement with a moisture problem needs that solved first, as our basement carpet guide lays out.

Carpet Padding: What Homeowners Ask Us

What thickness should carpet padding be?
For most residential cut-pile carpet, about 7/16 of an inch is the sweet spot, and anything past half an inch starts causing problems — flexing seams, loosening stretch, spongy stairs. Berber and other looped carpets want thinner and firmer still. Thickness is only half the spec, though: density is what determines how the pad performs over time.
Is thicker carpet padding better?
No — that is the most expensive misconception in the pad aisle. A thick, low-density pad feels plush in a showroom squeeze and collapses in the traffic lanes within a few years, taking the carpet with it. Past about half an inch, extra thickness actively works against the installation. Buy density at the correct thickness, not maximum thickness.
What density should carpet padding have?
Density — the pounds of material per cubic foot — is the spec that predicts how a pad holds up. Higher-density pads keep pushing back after years of footsteps; low-density foam quits early. Rather than memorizing numbers, stand on the grades with your carpet over them: the difference your feet feel in four seconds is the one your floor lives with for a decade.
Can I reuse my existing carpet pad under new carpet?
Almost never worth it. Pad compresses invisibly along every traffic lane, so the “fine-looking” old pad gives your new carpet the exact worn support map of the old one — and any history of pets or spills lives in it permanently. Against the cost of the whole job, new pad is a small line that protects the biggest one.
What is a moisture-barrier pad and do I need one?
A pad with a sealed top layer that stops liquid from soaking through to the pad and subfloor. Spills and accidents stay up in the carpet where cleaning can actually reach them, instead of becoming the permanent odor that ordinary sponge-style pad guarantees. In homes with pets or young kids, we consider it non-negotiable; elsewhere it is cheap insurance.
Does carpet padding affect the carpet warranty?
Yes. Many manufacturers specify the pad thickness and minimum density their warranties accept, and an over-thick or under-spec pad can quietly disqualify a claim before the carpet ever has a problem. It is one more reason the pad grade should be named in your quote, not hidden inside the word “standard.”
Is padding included in your installed carpet price?
Yes — our installed price starts at $1.49 per square foot with material, pad and labor together, and the written quote names which of the three grades that number includes. Upgrading a grade, or adding the moisture-barrier option, moves the total modestly. We bring all three grades to the estimate so you choose with your feet, not from a brochure.
What padding is best for stairs?
Dense and firm, on the thinner side of the range. Stairs concentrate more traffic per square foot than any room, and a soft, thick pad lets the carpet round over the nosing, wear at the fold, and feel vague underfoot — a safety issue as much as a wear issue. Firm pad keeps every tread edge crisp for the life of the installation.
