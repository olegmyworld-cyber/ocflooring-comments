What a Refinish Costs Before We Sand a Single Foot

Walk through what happens on every refinish, of any size. A truck is loaded with several hundred pounds of equipment — big sander, edger, the Lägler TRIO for the final pass, vacuums, finish, abrasives — driven to your house, and unloaded. The work area gets prepped and masked, adjacent spaces protected. Then the part people forget: a full sand-and-finish isn’t one visit. The finish system goes on in 2–3 coats with abrasion between them, so the job spans 3–5 days with a crew returning each day a coat goes down. Then teardown, load-out, disposal of spent abrasives and dust. Behind that sit the unglamorous fixed costs of doing this legally and well — licensing, insurance, a warranty that means something.

Almost none of that scales with square footage. Sanding 300 sq ft instead of 1,000 saves machine hours, but the load-in, the setup, the masking, the daily returns, and the drive to Lynnwood are identical. Priced at a flat $3.99/sq ft, a 300-foot job would gross $1,197 across three or four crew days — which is how a company ends up cutting the corners you can’t see. The minimum exists so the small job gets the same process as the big one.

Don’t Eat the Minimum — Use It

The move that turns the minimum from a tax into a bargain: every foot between your actual footage and 500 is the cheapest refinishing you will ever buy, because you’re paying for it whether you use it or not. If your worn area is 350 sq ft and the adjacent bedroom or hallway is due in the next few years anyway, doing it now costs nothing extra — the job was billing as 500 regardless. Stopping a sander at a doorway to save 150 sq ft you’ve already paid for is the least rational decision in flooring, and homeowners make it weekly.

Stairs are the other lever. Treads price per step at $55–$75 rather than by area, so a small-footage main level plus a flight of stairs becomes a properly sized project with no wasted minimum — and stairs are usually the most worn wood in the house anyway. We broke down that pricing in how stair refinishing is priced. And if the rest of your floor is dull but not damaged, pairing the full sand in the worn area with a $1.99/sq ft screen & recoat elsewhere can bring the whole floor to one even sheen for less than sanding everything — the difference between those two services is in our sand-and-refinish vs screen-and-recoat guide.

Smart ways to fill a small job

  • Add the adjacent room or hall you’d refinish in a few years anyway — those feet are already paid for
  • Add the stairs at $55–$75/tread; they’re priced per step, not per foot
  • Screen & recoat the rest of the floor at $1.99/sq ft for one uniform result
  • Coordinate with a neighbor’s project for the same week — shared mobilization helps everyone’s schedule
  • Wait and bundle: if nothing else is close to due, doing one room now and another next year buys two minimums

False economies to avoid

  • Hiring whoever quotes tiny jobs cheapest — the fixed costs didn’t vanish; the process did
  • Refinishing one room now and its twin in two years — two mobilizations, two minimums, one seam
  • Stopping at a doorway inside footage you’ve already paid for
  • DIY-sanding a small room to dodge the minimum — rental-machine damage costs more to fix than the minimum ever was
  • Assuming a low per-foot teaser price has no minimum or add-ons underneath — ask

Reading Other Companies’ Quotes With This Lens

Once you understand fixed costs, quote-shopping gets clearer. Every legitimate refinishing outfit handles small jobs somehow — a stated minimum like ours, a mobilization fee, a higher small-job rate, or a per-foot price quietly padded for everyone. The ones to squint at are quotes that seem to defy the economics: a rock-bottom rate with no minimum usually means corners are pre-cut — fewer coats, cheaper finish, one marathon day instead of a proper cure schedule. Job Minimums and Small Projects: What Lynnwood Homeowners Ask

What is your minimum job size for refinishing?
500 square feet. A project below that bills as 500 square feet at the standard rate — $3.99 per square foot for a full sand with natural finish, so about $1,995. Stair work is separate, priced per tread at $55 to $75, and doesn't need to meet the square-footage minimum on its own.
Why do small refinishing jobs cost more per square foot?
Because most refinishing costs are fixed: loading and transporting industrial equipment, setup and masking, return visits across 3 to 5 days for each coat of finish, teardown, and disposal. Those are identical for 300 or 1,300 square feet, so on a small job they're spread over fewer feet.
Is a minimum charge just profit padding?
No — it's what keeps a small job from being done badly. A crew that mobilizes a multi-day process for a few hundred dollars has to recover costs somewhere, and it usually comes out of coats, materials, or prep you can't see. The minimum funds the same process on a small job as a large one.
Can I refinish just one room?
Yes — it simply bills at the 500 square foot minimum if the room is smaller. Most clients use the gap instead of eating it: adding an adjacent hall or bedroom, or the stairs. Any footage between your room and 500 square feet is effectively free to include.
Do stairs count toward the 500 sq ft minimum?
Stairs are priced per tread at $55 to $75 rather than by area, so they're a separate line item — but practically, adding a staircase turns a too-small floor project into a right-sized visit, which is the outcome the minimum exists to protect.
Does the minimum apply to screen and recoat too?
The same mobilization logic applies, though screen and recoat is a one-day service at $1.99 per square foot, so its economics are gentler. It also pairs well with a small full-sand area: sand the damaged room, recoat the rest, one visit, one even floor.
My hallway is only 200 sq ft but it's the only worn part. What should I do?
Two honest options. If the surrounding floor is the same wood and roughly the same color, sand the hall and screen-and-recoat the rest for uniformity. If the rest of the floor is genuinely fine, consider waiting until more of the floor is due and doing it as one properly sized project — one mobilization instead of two.
Do all flooring companies have minimums?
Nearly all handle small jobs somehow: a stated minimum, a trip or mobilization fee, or a higher rate below a threshold. A company that claims no minimum and a bargain rate is recovering fixed costs invisibly — usually from the job itself. We'd rather state the number out loud.
