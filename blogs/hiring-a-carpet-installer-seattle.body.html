Why Carpet Quotes Are So Hard to Compare

Search “carpet installers near me” from a Seattle zip code and you will get national chains, big-box subcontract programs, one-truck independents, and a few companies like ours in between. Ask three of them to quote the same three bedrooms and a staircase and you can get numbers spread apart by four figures — on what sounds like the same job. The spread is almost never the carpet. It is everything wrapped around the carpet: which pad the number quietly assumes, whether the old floor's removal and disposal are inside or outside the price, whether the stairs were counted as stairs, and how the installer plans to attach the carpet to your floor.

None of that shows up in a per-square-foot teaser rate, which is why the teaser rate is the least useful line in any ad. The Pad Question Does the Most Work

If you only ask one question, ask about pad. It is the invisible half of the floor and the easiest place to cheapen a quote, because no homeowner has ever stood in a showroom comparing pads. The cheapest grade flattens within a few years and takes the carpet's appearance with it — fibers crush against a dead pad the way a mattress fails over a broken box spring. The difference between builder-grade and a proper mid-grade pad is small money on a whole-house job and enormous in year five.

A serious installer will name the pad — thickness, density, whether it carries a moisture barrier — and will let you stand on the options. We carry all three grades in the truck for exactly this reason, and the differences are obvious underfoot in seconds. Our guide to carpet padding thickness and density goes deeper, but at hiring time you only need the discipline: no pad name, no signature.

Power-Stretching Is Where Cheap Labor Hides

Carpet is supposed to be installed under tension, stretched drum-tight across the room with a power stretcher — a pole-and-lever tool that spans wall to wall — and hooked onto the tack strip at that tension. The shortcut is a knee-kicker alone: faster, no tool to haul in, and the carpet looks identical on day one. The difference arrives in year two or three, when the un-stretched carpet relaxes, and waves appear in the hallway. At that point you are paying someone to come back and restretch the carpet — fixing labor you already paid for once.

This is a one-question filter. Ask “do you power-stretch every room?” and listen. Anyone who does it will say yes instantly, because it is how the work is supposed to be done and they are proud of it. Hedging — “where it is needed,” “kickers are fine for bedrooms” — tells you where the low price came from.

Stairs, Closets, and Why the Measure Is the Estimate

Seattle housing is unkind to lazy math. A Wallingford craftsman has a staircase with a landing and a turn; a Columbia City remodel has closets nobody counted; a Belltown condo has an elevator to reserve and building hours to respect. Carpet also comes off fixed-width rolls — twelve feet, usually — so a room's price depends on how it cuts from the roll and where the seams land, not just its area. Two rooms of identical square footage can need meaningfully different amounts of carpet. That is why we treat measuring for carpet as the real work of the estimate, and why any quote produced without a tape measure in your hallway is a placeholder.

Stairs deserve their own line because they are their own trade. Each tread is wrapped or capped individually, tensioned by hand, and secured for safety — slow work with no straight runs to speed it up, which is why honest pricing is per tread. It is also why the fiber on stairs matters more than anywhere else in the house; our guide to carpet for stairs covers that choice. When an installer's quote shows treads counted, closets counted, and a waste allowance that reflects your actual room shapes, you are looking at someone who plans jobs rather than survives them.

Hiring a Carpet Installer in Seattle: What People Ask

How do I find a good carpet installer in Seattle?
Make every candidate answer five questions in writing: which pad grade the price assumes, whether every room is power-stretched, whether stairs are priced per tread, whether tear-out and haul-away are included, and whether they measure in your home. Good installers answer all five without flinching. Reviews and referrals narrow the list; these questions finish the job.
What should carpet installation cost in Seattle?
With us, installed carpet — material, pad and labor — starts at $1.49 per square foot, and the final number moves with fiber choice, pad grade, stair count and removal. Be suspicious of any precise total quoted before someone has measured your actual rooms, because roll widths and seam planning change what a room really needs.
Is a licensed installer really necessary for carpet?
In Washington, contractors are required to be registered, bonded and insured, and you can verify all three in the state contractor database in about a minute. Beyond the legal point, the credential is a cheap filter: someone who skipped registration has told you how they handle the parts of the job you cannot see.
What is power-stretching and why does it matter?
A power stretcher spans the room and tensions the carpet properly before it is hooked to the tack strip. Carpet installed with only a knee-kicker looks identical at first, then relaxes into ripples within a few years — a failure you pay to fix later. Asking "do you power-stretch every room?" is the fastest way to sort installers.
Should the old carpet removal be included in the quote?
It should at least be a clear written line: tear-out of carpet, pad, staples and tack strip, plus haul-away and disposal. A house of old carpet is heavy and bulky, and dump fees are real, so vague quotes love to leave it out and rediscover it on install day. Ours includes it in the written number.
How are stairs priced for carpet?
Per tread. Each step is wrapped or capped individually and secured by hand, so a staircase carries far more labor per square foot than any room. A quote that folds stairs into total square footage is either underpricing careful work or hiding margin — either way, ask for the treads as their own line.
Why do you bring samples to the house instead of having a store?
Because carpet color is a lighting decision and stores have the wrong lighting. Twenty-plus full-size samples on your own floor, in your own light, next to your own paint, beats any showroom — and while we are there we measure every room and stair, let you stand on all three pad grades, and leave a written price the same visit.
How long does carpet installation take once I hire someone?
Most Seattle homes install in a single day once the carpet arrives from the mill; ordering is usually the longer wait. Staircases, large homes and condo logistics — elevator reservations, building work hours — can add time, and a good installer tells you which category your job is in at the estimate, not on the morning.
