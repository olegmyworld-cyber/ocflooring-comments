Every change below is **staged in Webflow and not published**. The live site still shows the old version until someone clicks Publish. Read the "Before you publish" section first.

Site: Webflow `66cc1f4542ee0c42e43d0303` (willsflooring.com). Source audit: `AUDIT-2026-09-08.md`. Keyword evidence: `keyword-map.md`. Applied page titles: `page-settings.csv`. Required redirects: `redirects-todo.txt`.

## Before you publish

1. **Add the 7 redirects in `redirects-todo.txt`** (Webflow → Site Settings → Publishing → 301 Redirects). Seven page URLs changed. Without the redirects those old URLs return 404 the moment you publish.
2. **Look at the site on the Webflow preview**, especially the Why We're Different page (rebuilt), the Pricing page (now visible in the nav) and the footer (now carries phone, email and address). Nothing here was checked in a browser: this sandbox cannot reach willsflooring.com.
3. **Check the reviews widget position.** An Elfsight widget div was sitting inside `<head>`, which is invalid HTML. It moved to the site footer. It will now appear at the bottom of every page rather than wherever the browser previously forced it. If it looks wrong, delete the div line from Site Settings → Custom Code → Footer and keep the loader script.
4. **The Home page had unpublished edits from before this work started.** Publishing ships those too. Worth a look.

## Verification

A read-only pass re-checked the claims above against the Webflow API on 9 Sep. Confirmed: the site's structured data parses and holds one business with the Everett address and Mon–Fri hours; ten sampled city pages each have one Service entry whose URL matches the page's real address; the Terms heading is fixed; the Why page shows seven distinct reasons with no "OC Flooring" text; Contact shows the Everett address; both new service pages exist with full content; the FAQ has 15 entries.

Two real problems were found and fixed: the ten new FAQ entries were still in the sitemap even though their template page is blank, and the Why page's structured data still listed four reasons where the page now shows seven.

**A warning for whoever works on this next.** The Webflow element query reports `linkType: none` for every link that points at a page rather than a URL. It looks exactly like a broken link and it is not. The first verification pass reported the booking link dead on all 128 city pages, the Pricing nav link hidden, and seven Pricing page buttons unlinked. All three were false alarms: reading the raw element settings shows `{"mode":"page","to":{"pageId":"…"}}` in every case, and the Pricing nav link is set visible with a static value. The nav visibility prop still exists but nothing reads it any more. Check raw settings before "fixing" a link that already works.

## Known limits of this work

- Nothing was verified in a browser. The sandbox cannot reach willsflooring.com. All checks were made against the Webflow API.
- Page speed, mobile layout and the external links (Google Maps, Wells Fargo application) were not tested.
- Two Financing paragraphs are still H4 headings used as body text. No API converts a heading to a paragraph.
- The "View All Products" button on /products links to itself. It sits in a component used on 131 pages, so retargeting it would break the others.
- The "Save Up to $1,000" nav banner still has no terms stated anywhere on the site. Left as is; it is your call.
- The flooring calculator still loads an unpinned third-party script (`webflow-tools@latest`).
- City page slugs still carry legacy junk on many pages ("expert-hardwood-floor-installation-in-arlington-wa-quality-craftsmanship"). Renaming each needs its own redirect and was out of scope.
- Refinishing pages still say "more than 12 years of hardwood refinishing experience" in body copy where the canonical figure is 13 years. The sentence wraps inline bold, which the editing API would flatten, so it was left for a manual pass.
- The 128 city pages still do not link to each other. A customer on the Lynnwood laminate page has no route to the Lynnwood refinishing page. That is the largest remaining SEO opportunity and it needs a template change, not a text edit.
